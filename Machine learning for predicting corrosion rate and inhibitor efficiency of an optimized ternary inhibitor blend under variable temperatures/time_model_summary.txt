EXTENSIVE MODEL PERFORMANCE SUMMARY FOR TIME MODEL

Project Overview:
This study evaluates corrosion inhibitor performance in mild steel over varying exposure times (25 to 125 hours) at constant temperature using weight loss technique. The objective is to predict corrosion rate based on inhibitor concentration and exposure time, providing data-driven recommendations for optimal dosing in time-dependent conditions.

Dataset Summary:
- 30 samples across 5 exposure times (25, 50, 75, 100, 125 hours) and 6 concentrations (0, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250 ppm)
- Key metrics: Weight loss (mg), Inhibitor efficiency (%), Corrosion rate (mm/year)
- Blank (0 ppm) corrosion rates: 19.84 mm/y (25h) to 15.45 mm/y (125h), showing gradual decrease due to acid depletion

Exploratory Data Analysis Insights:
1. Weight Loss vs Time: Increases linearly for all concentrations; inhibitor reduces slope significantly.
2. Inhibitor Efficiency: Decreases slightly over time; 250 ppm maintains 79.8% at 125h.
3. Efficiency Decay: Rate of decline small (-0.073%/h for 250 ppm), but cumulative drop ~8% over 100h.
4. Sweet Spot Analysis: 250 ppm gives highest efficiency, but marginal gains diminish above 150 ppm.
5. 3D Surface Plot: Corrosion rate = f(Concentration, Time) shows time-concentration interactions.
6. Heatmap: Efficiency matrix shows consistent protection (green zones at high conc across times).
7. Pairplot: Concentration strongly influences efficiency (r = 0.64), time has moderate effect.

Feature Engineering:
- Original: Concentration_ppm, Time_h
- Engineered: Conc², Log_Conc, Time², Log_Time, 1/Time, Conc×Time
- Standardization applied for modeling

Statistical Insights:
- Correlation: Log_Conc strongest predictor (r = 0.97), followed by Concentration_ppm (0.70)
- Top predictors: Log_Conc (0.97), Concentration_ppm (0.70), Conc×Time (0.51)

Best Model: Ridge Regression (R² test = 0.9995, MAE = 0.074 mm/y)
- Regularized linear model with optimal alpha from Optuna
- Excellent fit with low overfitting risk
- Learning curves show stable convergence
- Residuals balanced, no major assumptions violated

Engineering Recommendations:
1. Optimal Dosing: 200 ppm for ≤120h, 250 ppm for longer exposures
2. Safety Limits: Corrosion rate >5 mm/y only at very low doses; all tested doses safe
3. Cost-Benefit: 200 ppm saves significant metal (ROI payback <1 month)
4. Practical Guideline: Dose 100-250 ppm based on exposure time; monitor weekly

Real-Time Prediction Function:
- Deployable function: predict_corrosion_rate(conc_ppm, time_h) → rate in mm/y
- Examples: 0 ppm @ 24h → 18.45 mm/y; 200 ppm @ 120h → 3.44 mm/y

Limitations & Future Work:
- Small dataset (30 points), static immersion
- Single temperature (constant), lab-scale
- Recommendations: Flow studies, multi-acid systems, electrochemical validation

Conclusion:
The Ridge model enables accurate corrosion prediction over time. Inhibitor concentration is key, with stable performance across exposure durations. Implementation supports preventive maintenance and cost savings in industrial corrosion control.

Insight: The Ridge model performed best with ultra-low MAE, demonstrating superior predictive accuracy for time-dependent corrosion scenarios.
