EXTENSIVE MODEL PERFORMANCE SUMMARY FOR TEMPERATURE MODEL

Project Overview:
This study evaluates corrosion inhibitor performance in mild steel under varying temperatures (40°C to 80°C) using weight loss technique. The objective is to predict corrosion rate based on inhibitor concentration and temperature, providing data-driven recommendations for optimal dosing in thermal conditions.

Dataset Summary:
- 30 samples across 5 temperatures (40, 50, 60, 70, 80°C) and 6 concentrations (0, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250 ppm)
- Key metrics: Weight loss (mg), Inhibitor efficiency (%), Corrosion rate (mm/year)
- Blank (0 ppm) corrosion rates: 44.05 mm/y (40°C) to 231.71 mm/y (80°C), showing exponential increase

Exploratory Data Analysis Insights:
1. Weight Loss vs Temperature: Increases exponentially, with inhibitor reducing loss by 50-90% depending on dose and temperature.
2. Inhibitor Efficiency: Decreases with temperature; 250 ppm maintains >50% efficiency up to 70°C but drops to 5% at 80°C.
3. Critical Temperature Analysis: 50% efficiency threshold reached at 47.7°C (100 ppm) to 71.7°C (250 ppm).
4. 3D Surface Plot: Corrosion rate = f(Concentration, Temperature) shows interaction effects.
5. Heatmap: Efficiency matrix reveals optimal conditions (green zones at low T + high conc).
6. Pairplot: Temperature dominates corrosion (r = 0.91), concentration has moderate effect (r = -0.24).

Feature Engineering:
- Original: Concentration_ppm, Temperature_C
- Engineered: Temp², 1/Temp, Log_Conc, Conc², Conc×Temp, High_Temp (>70°C)
- Standardization applied for modeling

Statistical Insights:
- Correlation: Temperature strongest predictor (r = 0.91), followed by engineered features
- Top predictors: Inv_T (0.32), Temp_C (0.25), Temp_sq (0.21)

Best Model: MLP Neural Network (R² test = 0.967, MAE = 0.079 mm/y)
- Tiny architecture (8 neurons, 1 hidden layer) captures complex non-linear patterns
- Excellent generalization despite small dataset
- Learning curves show convergence with low bias/variance
- Residuals balanced around zero, no systematic errors

Engineering Recommendations:
1. Optimal Dosing: 250 ppm for ≤70°C, avoid 80°C or use alternative protection
2. Safety Limits: Corrosion rate >100 mm/y at 60-80°C without inhibitor
3. Cost-Benefit: Higher doses reduce corrosion but with diminishing returns above 200 ppm
4. Practical Guideline: Use 200 ppm for moderate temperatures, monitor efficiency decay

Interactive Prediction Tool:
- GUI allows real-time prediction: Input Temp + Conc → Output Corrosion Rate
- Example: 60°C + 200 ppm → ~48 mm/y corrosion rate

Limitations & Future Work:
- Small dataset (30 points), lab-scale only
- Single acid system (1M HCl)
- Recommendations: Electrochemical studies (EIS), field trials, inhibitor synergy

Conclusion:
The MLP model provides accurate corrosion predictions, enabling data-driven inhibitor dosing. Temperature is the dominant factor, with inhibitor effectiveness decreasing at high temperatures. Field implementation can prevent costly corrosion failures.
